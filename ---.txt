## Overview

Zomato’s visual language is bold, direct, and highly appetizing, with a cinematic dark backdrop that lets the food photography dominate. The tone feels premium but approachable: big, confident typography, minimal chrome, and fast-to-scan calls to action. It is optimized for conversion on a broad consumer audience, especially people ready to order quickly.

## Colors

- **Primary (#FFFFFF):** Bright white is the dominant text and CTA color, used for headlines, body copy, and button surfaces where maximum contrast is needed.
- **Secondary (#1C1C1C):** Deep charcoal anchors the page background and provides the dark stage that makes imagery and white type pop.
- **Tertiary (#FF4D4D):** A vivid red accent suitable for promotional emphasis and brand moments; use sparingly so it retains energy.
- **Neutral (#F5F5F5):** Soft off-white for subtle surfaces and muted supporting elements when pure white would feel too stark.
- **Surface (#FFFFFF):** Clean white used for cards, primary buttons, and elevated content areas.
- **On-surface (#1C1C1C):** Near-black text color for use on white surfaces and any light UI container.
- **Border (#FFEDEF):** Very light blush border tone that works best for soft card framing or delicate separators.
- **Error (#E53935):** Reserved red for validation and destructive states, distinct from the brand accent.

## Typography

The system uses **Our Lexend** throughout, creating a friendly geometric feel that stays legible over photography. Headings are strong and compact, mostly set in 600 weight, with the largest display style used for the hero message and slightly smaller sizes for supporting hierarchy. Body copy is clean and neutral at 16px, while labels and buttons stay simple and utilitarian rather than overly styled.

Letter spacing is set to zero across the system, which keeps the brand voice straightforward and modern. There is no uppercase-heavy convention visible in the source; instead, emphasis comes from size, weight, and stacking. Use bold, centered headline treatment for hero messaging and keep supporting text lighter and smaller for quick scanning.

## Layout & Spacing

The page is centered and vertically layered over a full-bleed image, with a strong middle-aligned content stack. Spacing is generous at the hero level: the system relies on large jumps between the headline, subheadline, buttons, and footer prompt to preserve clarity against a busy background. Use the spacing scale in broad steps rather than tight incremental spacing, especially for large landing-page compositions.

The rhythm is intentionally spacious and theatrical, with large section spacing tokens such as 78px and 152px for major breaks. Smaller controls and buttons use compact internal padding so the interface feels efficient without looking crowded. Keep content blocks narrow and centered when overlaid on imagery to maintain a clear reading lane.

## Elevation & Depth

The interface is mostly flat, with very little shadow-based hierarchy. Depth comes from tonal contrast: white text and white cards/buttons sit on a dark background, while the hero photography provides the primary visual richness. When elevation is needed, use subtle shadows only; they should support readability rather than introduce a layered dashboard feel.

Borders are light and restrained, especially around cards and outlined buttons. Avoid heavy blur, soft glows, or complex layered shadows, since the design relies on crisp contrast and strong image composition instead.

## Shapes

The shape language is simple and slightly rounded, with 4px corners for most interactive elements. Cards are more softened at 16px, which makes them feel friendly without becoming playful. Overall, the geometry is practical and minimal, with full pills reserved for chips and highly rounded affordances.

## Components

Buttons are compact and high-contrast. `button-primary` uses a white fill, dark text, 8px 16px padding, 40px height, and 120px minimum width; it should feel like the most actionable control. `button-secondary` is the inverse, using a transparent background, white border, and white text for use on dark imagery. `button-link` is plain text with underline treatment and no container, appropriate for low-emphasis navigation or legal links.

Cards should feel clean and lightly elevated, with white backgrounds, 16px radii, thin borders, and modest padding. Keep card content dark on light surfaces and avoid decorative shadows beyond a subtle soft lift. Inputs should follow the same restrained pattern: white surface, 4px radius, compact padding, and clear contrast for text and placeholder content.

Chips and small tags should be pill-shaped with full rounding and compact horizontal padding. They can use the dark background with white text to match the site’s high-contrast identity. Any icon-text clusters, such as app store badges or footer prompts, should remain tight and horizontally balanced rather than boxed into large containers.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do keep the interface centered and image-led, with the hero content stacked in a narrow readable column.
- Do use white text and white controls against dark imagery for the strongest brand expression.
- Do preserve the spacious vertical rhythm; let headings, CTA buttons, and footers breathe.
- Do keep corners modest on buttons and inputs, and slightly softer on cards.
- Don't introduce heavy shadows, glassmorphism, or complex gradients that compete with the photography.
- Don't use many colors in the UI; the system should stay mostly monochrome with red as a restrained accent.
- Don't overcomplicate typography with multiple fonts, uppercase styling, or decorative treatments.
- Don't make buttons oversized or overly pill-shaped; the design should stay compact and efficient.
